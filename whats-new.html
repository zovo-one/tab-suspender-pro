What's new in 1.0.37

Nothing on this list needs a setting. Your whitelist, timer and profile carry over exactly as you left them.

New

A tab wakes up where you left it

Scroll position is saved when a tab goes to sleep and put back when it comes back, so a long article opens at the paragraph you stopped on.

Back never lands on the sleeping page

Waking a tab no longer adds a history entry. The Back button goes to the page before, the way it did before the tab slept.

Native mode

Turn it on in Settings and the extension uses Chrome's own discard instead of a placeholder page. The tab keeps its title and icon, uses no memory, and reloads when you click it.

Pause for 1 hour, 8 hours or until tomorrow

Pick one from the popup when you need every tab live, for a demo or a long research session. Suspending starts again on its own.

Protect one tab for this session

One click in the popup keeps a single tab awake until you close it, with no whitelist rule left behind.

Improved

Only suspend when more than N tabs are open

Light tab users never see a sleeping tab. The default is 0, which means always, and you set the number in Settings.

Timers keep working past 500 tabs

They also survive Chrome restarting the extension in the background, which used to leave some tabs awake for good.

The sleeping page has a keyboard

Enter wakes the tab, and a "Never suspend this site" link adds the domain to your whitelist without a trip to Settings.

Alt+S works with the default settings

The rule that keeps the tab you are reading awake used to block your own command. "Suspend this tab" and the shortcut now go through.

Fixed

Pasting a key works the first time

The field no longer doubles the ZOVO- prefix or cuts it short, and you can clear it.

Settings shows the real version and your real counts

The invented "top 1% of users" rank and the countdown banner are gone.

For Pro members

URL wildcard patterns

Rules such as https://docs.google.com/* or *://*.atlassian.net/* keep whole paths awake, not just a domain. Free users keep the 5-domain whitelist.

Pro stays on while you are offline

Only a definite "invalid" answer from the server turns it off, after a 7-day grace period. A server outage or a closed laptop no longer does.

Questions people ask

Why did a tab not fall asleep?

The timer starts over every time you click, type or scroll in a tab, so a tab only sleeps after it has sat untouched for the full time you chose. Tabs that are playing audio, pinned, on your whitelist or holding text you typed into a form stay awake on purpose, and so does the tab you are looking at. If you set a minimum tab count, nothing sleeps until you have more tabs than that open.

Browser pages such as chrome:// addresses, other extensions and local files never sleep either. If a normal tab still will not go, open Settings and check the protections, or email support@zovo.one with the site.

What happens to a tab when it goes to sleep?

A sleeping tab keeps its title and address. Clicking it loads the page again from the web and scrolls back to where you were. Anything unsaved on the page starts fresh. The timer skips tabs where you have typed into a regular form field, but editors like Gmail, Slack or Notion are not detected, so send or save a draft before you step away.

One catch. Sleeping tabs need the extension to wake up, so press Alt+R before you disable or remove it.

What does Native mode change?

Chrome discards the tab itself instead of showing the sleeping page. The tab keeps its title and icon in the tab strip and uses no memory until you click it, at which point Chrome reloads it. You lose the sleeping page and its "Never suspend this site" link, and scroll position comes back only when the site restores it on its own.

Does Tab Suspender Pro send my browsing anywhere?

No. Suspending, waking and the numbers on this page all happen inside your browser. The one exception is Pro. About once a day it confirms your license key with Zovo's server, sending only the key and the extension's name.

What happens if I stop paying for Pro?

Pro keeps working until the end of the month you paid for. After that you're on the free version again, with your tabs and settings where you left them, and the Pro-only options lock until you come back.
